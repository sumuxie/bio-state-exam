## Findings

### 10-21 — CONTRADICTS-NODE — severity: medium
**The field says (`mustKnow`):** "cortisol, a steroid from the cortex, **for sustained
stress**, and adrenaline, an amino-acid derivative from the medulla, for short-term stress"
**And (`summary`):** "both are stress hormones, but cortisol answers **sustained** stress and
adrenaline short-term stress, **which the table states explicitly**."
**The node says (`points[1]`):** "Cortisol, adrenal cortex: **response to stress**, increase of
blood pressure and sugar."
**And (`points[7]`):** "cortisol (steroid, cortex) **for stress in general**, adrenalin
(amino-acid derivative, medulla) **explicitly** for SHORT-TERM stress."
**Why these cannot both hold:** the transcribed row for cortisol carries no time qualifier at
all. `points[7]` is careful about exactly this — only adrenalin's "short-term" is the table's
own word — and then `summary` asserts the contrast is what "the table states explicitly", and
`mustKnow` promotes "sustained" to a fact about cortisol. The `oral` model repeats it ("one for
acute stress and one sustained"), while the `oral` checklist correctly asks only for adrenalin's
short-term label. A candidate asked "which does Tab. 10.1 assign to sustained stress?" would
answer from the book's silence.
**Resolvable from the node alone?** yes — `points[1]` and `points[7]` already carry the
book's actual wording; only `mustKnow`, `summary` and the `oral` model overreach.

### 10-4 — UNSOURCED — severity: low (number likely wrong as well)
**The field says (`mustKnow`):** "the whole-body pool is **a few grams**, yet the body makes
about 70 kg of it in 24 hours"
**The node says (`coverageNote`):** "Both quantitative claims were read as printed:
concentrations of the order of mmol/l, and the calculation that the human body produces about
70 kg of ATP in 24 hours."
**Why this is a finding:** the node's own record of what is on the page lists two numbers, and
"a few grams" is not one of them. It appears in `mustKnow` and in `summary` ("A standing pool of
a few grams") and nowhere in `points`, `terms` or `coverageNote`. The standing figure usually
quoted is ~50 g, an order of magnitude more, so this is not a harmless illustration. The
internal arithmetic does close (a few grams × thousands of turnovers ≈ 70 kg), so this is not
INTERNAL-NUMBERS; it is a number arriving from outside the node.
**Resolvable from the node alone?** no — needs p.219 to confirm the book gives no standing-pool
mass; if it does not, the phrasing should say so.

### 10-9, 10-19, 10-20 — QUIZ-REFERENCE (outside the seven classes) — severity: low

## Needs the book (not internal-consistency findings)

- **10-11, the GTP.** The node states in five places — `coverageNote`, `summary`, `points[4]`,
  `mustKnow` (implicitly) and the `oral` checklist ("Gave the GTP and located it at the
  α-oxoglutarate oxidative decarboxylation") — that the macroergic GTP is formed *during the
  oxidative decarboxylation of α-oxoglutarate*. The GTP is actually made in the following step,
  by succinyl-CoA synthetase. This is very likely the book's own compression, faithfully
  transcribed, and it would be flagged if so — the node flags exactly this kind of compression
  in 10-6 (key point 2) and 10-8. It is the one place where a comparable compression passes
  without a note. Worth a look at pp.220–221; the fix, if the book says it, is a `coverageNote`
  line, not a content change.
- **10-22, the relaxin row.** "Relaxin, **ovary**: increases the motility of **sperm**" is
  reproduced without comment, and it is the one row in 27 that reads as though something were
  garbled. It is defensible (relaxin does occur in seminal plasma and raises sperm motility),
  so I would not change it — but since the node comments on every other oddity in the table,
  a one-line check of p.225 would settle whether the organ column really says *vaječník*.
- **10-4, "a few grams".** See finding 2 — needs p.219 to confirm the book gives no
  standing-pool mass.
