### `4-2-4-1` — CONTRADICTS-NODE — severity: high

**The field says** (`points[6]`, trp leader sequence): "RNA-polymerase first transcribes only a
LEADER SEQUENCE (trpL, 162 nucleotides) — transcribed into mRNA but **NEVER translated into
peptide, a fact that holds generally, not just for trp**."

**The node says** (`points[7]`, attenuation mechanism): "Attenuation (obr. 4.49) is **controlled
by a ribosome translating the leader peptide** just behind the transcribing RNA-polymerase."
And `mustKnow`: "a **translating ribosome's own position on the leader mRNA** decides, in real
time, whether tryptophan is scarce enough to justify finishing the transcript."
And `quiz[3].answer_en`: "a short **leader peptide** with tandem Trp codons **is translated** by a
ribosome that trails the RNA-polymerase."

**Why these cannot both hold:** `points[6]` says the leader is never translated into peptide;
`points[7]`, the `mustKnow` and the quiz all require a ribosome to be translating a leader
*peptide* there — that translation *is* the attenuation mechanism. `points[6]` also generalises
the false half ("a fact that holds generally"), so a reader meets the wrong statement first and in
its strongest form. Three of four places in the node say the leader is translated; `points[6]` is
the outlier.

Note that `oral.model_en` and `quiz[3].q_en` use the softer, defensible wording — "never
translated into **a protein of its own**" / "**a functional protein product of its own**" — which
is compatible with a short regulatory leader peptide being made. So the whole node except
`points[6]` is coherent; `points[6]` dropped the qualifier and added a generalisation.

**Resolvable from the node alone?** The internal inconsistency is resolvable — `points[7]` is
plainly right and is what the mechanism requires. Whether the Czech text itself makes this slip
needs p.104–105. This node flags two *other* book defects meticulously (see "already flagged"),
so if this one is the book's it is currently the only unflagged one on the page.

### `4-1-3-1` — CONTRADICTS-NODE — severity: medium

**The field says** (`points[8]`): "TWO FURTHER polymerases exist, DNA POLYMERASE II and III. Both
share the SAME TWO activities as pol I (polymerase + 3′→5′ exonuclease), but **DNA polymerase II
LACKS the 5′→3′ nuclease activity**."

**The node says** (`oral.model_en`): "Two further polymerases exist, **DNA polymerase two and
three**, sharing the polymerase and three-prime to five-prime exonuclease activities but **lacking
the five-prime to three-prime nuclease**."

**Why these cannot both hold:** `points[8]` names only polymerase II in the contrast clause, so it
reads as II-lacks-it-but-III-has-it; the oral model answer says both II and III lack it. This is
exactly the ambiguity flagged to me, and reading it in full context confirms it is real rather
than apparent: the contrast structure of `points[8]` ("Both share… **but** DNA polymerase II
lacks…") invites the reader to supply "…unlike III". Nothing else in the node resolves it —
`terms` gives DNA polymerase III's entry as "The cell's true DNA replicase — the only one of the
three polymerases with a demonstrated in vivo role", with no nuclease information at all, and
`quiz[1].why_en` repeats the singular form ("DNA polymerase II shares pol I's two enzymatic
activities but lacks the 5′→3′ nuclease").

Severity **medium** rather than high: the oral answer is unambiguous, so a candidate who works
through the whole node lands on the correct reading, and the correct reading is the biochemically
true one (E. coli Pol III has no 5′→3′ exonuclease; only Pol I does). But "does Pol III have
5′→3′ nuclease activity?" is a plausible exam item and `points[8]` alone answers it wrongly.

**Resolvable from the node alone?** Yes — `oral.model_en` is the correct side. A one-word repair
("but DNA polymerases II and III both LACK…") closes it without needing the book.

### `4-1-4-1` + `4-1-4-2` — INTERNAL-NUMBERS — severity: medium

This is the rRNA-size item I was asked to verify. **It is a real conflict, and neither node flags
it** — but it is cross-node, not within-node, and that distinction matters for how it should be
fixed, so I am reporting it with the qualification.

**`4-1-4-1` `points[4]`:** "PROKARYOTES have THREE sizes of rRNA, named by SEDIMENTATION
COEFFICIENT: 23S (≈1.2×10³ kDa, **≈3700 nucleotides**), 16S (≈0.5×10³ kDa, **≈1700 nt**) and 5S
(≈3.6×10¹ kDa, ≈120 nt) — Tab. 4.2, re-read directly from the scan to confirm the exponents."

**`4-1-4-2` `points[8]`:** "a single primary transcript is cut by ribonucleases … into the mature
**16S (1541 nt)**, **23S (2904 nt)** and 5S (120 nt) rRNAs plus several tRNAs."

**Why these cannot both hold:** these are the same two molecules in the same organism. 3700 vs
2904 is a 27% discrepancy and 1700 vs 1541 is 10% — far outside what the "≈" covers. E. coli 23S
rRNA is 2904 nt and 16S is 1541 nt, so `4-1-4-2` carries the exact values and `4-1-4-1` the
inflated ones.

**Each node is internally sound, which is why this survived.** `4-1-4-1`'s numbers close
arithmetically against its own mass column: 1.2×10³ kDa ÷ 3700 nt = 324 Da/nt, 36 kDa ÷ 120 nt =
300 Da/nt — both correct for a ribonucleotide residue. So the ≈3700 figure is a defensible
conversion *from the kDa column*, and nothing inside `4-1-4-1` exposes it. Working the other way,
2904 nt × ~321 Da = ~930 kDa, not 1.2×10³ kDa, so the book's own Tab. 4.2 mass and its own
Obr. 4.33 nucleotide counts do not agree with each other.

**This is aggravated by a quiz.** `4-1-4-1` `quiz[1]` asks "which rRNA species is the LARGEST in
E. coli, and roughly how many nucleotides", answer "23S rRNA, about 3700 nucleotides", with
`why_en` insisting on it: "at about 1.2×10³ kDa and **roughly 3700 nucleotides** — do not misread
the exponent as 10⁶ or 10⁹". So the reader is actively drilled on 3700 in one node and taught 2904
in the next.

**Resolvable from the node alone? No** — needs Tab. 4.2 on p.86 and Obr. 4.33 on p.91. Note that
`4-1-4-1`'s `coverageNote` claims verification only of "the exponents (×10³ kDa for 23S/16S, ×10¹
kDa for 5S/tRNA)", i.e. of the **mass** column; it does not claim the nucleotide counts were read
off the table. If Tab. 4.2 prints no nucleotide column, the ≈3700/≈1700 figures are this node's
own derivation and can simply be replaced with 2904/1541 plus a note that the printed masses are
high. If the table does print them, the book contradicts itself and both nodes need a
`coverageNote` line saying so.

### `4-1-2` — CONTRADICTS-NODE — severity: low

**The field says** (`mustKnow`): "Base pairing is not symmetric in strength: G-C holds three
hydrogen bonds, A-T only two, **so** a stretch of DNA rich in G-C is measurably harder to pull
apart than one rich in A-T".

**The node says** (`lehNotes[0]`, kind `conflict`): "Lehninger states the opposite explicitly:

**Why these cannot both hold:** the `mustKnow`'s *conclusion* (GC-rich DNA is harder to separate)

**Severity low, deliberately.** The lehNote is a cross-book note whose stated target is the node's
stabilisation ranking generally ("this node has the ranking the wrong way round"), so the node
does already flag the underlying disagreement; and for a state exam on the Czech book the
`mustKnow` gives the answer the examiner expects. I record it because the lehNote singles out this
exact inference as the trap, and the `mustKnow` — always visible, read before the lehNote — walks
into it. Listed rather than raised.

**Resolvable from the node alone?** Yes; it is a wording choice ("and, separately, GC-rich DNA is
harder to pull apart"), not a factual repair.

### `4-1-5` — LANGUAGE — severity: medium-high

**The field says** (`points[4]`, `en`): "Cleavage of the NUCLEOSIDE bond between sugar and base is
catalysed by the enzyme **NUCLEOSIDASE**, which acts mainly PHOSPHOROLYTICALLY, giving the free
base plus RIBOSE-PHOSPHATE."

**The Chinese half says:** "糖与碱基之间核苷键的断裂由**核苷酸酶**（nukleosidasa）催化，其作用方式主要是磷酸解…"
— 核苷酸酶 is **nucleotidase**, not nucleosidase (which is 核苷酶).

**Why this is the worst of the four:** it is not a translation nicety, it collapses two different
enzymes in this node onto one Chinese name. The immediately preceding `points[3]` uses the same
characters for the genuinely different enzyme — "生成的核苷酸会被**核苷酸酶**进一步切割，核苷酸酶本质上是磷酸酶" (en:
"The nucleotides produced are further cleaved by NUCLEOTIDASES, which are essentially
PHOSPHATASES") — and so does the `terms` entry. A Chinese reader therefore meets one enzyme name
performing two different reactions in adjacent points, and the two-alternative-fates structure the
node is built around becomes unreadable. **核苷酶 appears nowhere in either file**, so nothing
elsewhere rescues it; only the parenthetical Czech gloss `（nukleosidasa）` preserves the identity,
and the second occurrence has no gloss at all.

**Second occurrence, no gloss:** `quiz[3].q_en` "Once **nucleosidase** releases the free purine and
pyrimidine bases…" against `q_cn` "**核苷酸酶**释放出游离的嘌呤和嘧啶碱基之后…". By this node's own text,
nucleotidase yields nucleosides, not free bases — so the Chinese question states something the
node denies.

**Resolvable from the node alone?** Yes. Purely a Chinese-side term fix; the English is correct
throughout.

### `4-1-1-2` and `5-2-2-1` — LANGUAGE — severity: low-medium

**Systematic:** English "committed step" is rendered in Chinese as 限速 / 限速反应 / 限速步骤
("rate-limiting step") in **four places across both chapters**, and nowhere in either file does an
English half make a kinetic claim.

| node | field | en | cn |
|---|---|---|---|
| `4-1-1-2` | `summary` | "the **committed step** is feedback-inhibited by CTP" | "**限速的第一步**受 CTP 反馈抑制" |
| `4-1-1-2` | `terms` (ATCase) | "catalysing the first **committed step**" | "催化第一步**限速反应**" |
| `4-1-1-2` | `quiz[3].answer_cn` | "the allosteric enzyme catalysing the first **committed step**" | "催化第一步**限速反应**" |
| `5-2-2-1` | `quiz[1].why_` | "carbamoyl phosphate synthesis, a later, **committed step** of the urea cycle proper" | "氨甲酰磷酸的合成，是尿素循环本身后续的**限速步骤**" |

**Why these are different claims:** *committed step* is about irreversibility — the point past
which substrate cannot be diverted — and is the reason a pathway puts feedback inhibition there.
*限速步骤* is a kinetic statement about which step controls flux. The two often coincide but are not
the same, and only the Chinese reader is told the kinetic one.

Severity is graded: for ATCase in `4-1-1-2` the rate-limiting reading is at least defensible and
the node's own point ("classic FEEDBACK regulation") supports the regulatory role either way. The
`5-2-2-1` instance is the weaker one — `5-2-2-2` characterises carbamoyl phosphate synthetase only
as "STRONGLY ENDERGONIC" and "ACTIVATED by N-ACETYLGLUTAMATE", never as rate-limiting, so 限速步骤
there is an examinable assertion sourced nowhere in either node.

**Note on how this was found.** The ch4 sweep returned `4-1-1-2` clean; only the ch5 sweep flagged
the same substitution in `5-2-2-1`. Grepping the pattern across both files then turned up the three
ch4 instances. Worth recording as a method lesson: a per-chapter language sweep will miss a
translation habit that is consistent *within* a chapter, because nothing inside that chapter
contradicts it.

### `4-2-4-2` — LANGUAGE — severity: low

**The field says** (`points[8]`, `en`): "an **INVARIANT** sequence GU at the intron's 5′-edge and
an **equally INVARIANT** AG at its 3′-edge — **the book treats both boundaries as invariant, with
no difference in strictness between them** — these are NECESSARY AND SUFFICIENT to define the
splice site."

**The Chinese half says:** "剪接位点由内含子5′端**保守的**GU序列和3′端**恒定不变的**AG序列所定义——这两者是定义剪接位点的必要且充分条件。"

**How they differ:** the English carries an inserted clause written specifically to deny any
asymmetry between the two boundaries. The Chinese drops that clause and then grades them
differently anyway — 保守 ("conserved") for GU against 恒定不变 ("absolutely invariant") for AG —
reinstating exactly the asymmetry the English clause exists to rule out. Low severity: the
load-bearing GU…AG "necessary and sufficient" claim is identical on both sides, and this node's
`lehNotes` already flags "necessary and sufficient" as overstated regardless.
